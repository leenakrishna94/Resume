
# MASTER PROMPT — JOB-SPECIFIC RESUME RESEARCHER, WRITER, AND REVIEWER

Create a complete, technically detailed, professionally written, ATS-friendly resume tailored to the current job description.

Act from the perspectives of a technical recruiter, hiring manager, sector researcher, and resume writer. Make my relevant qualifications clear through substantive experience bullets, accurate technical terminology, and coherent professional positioning.

My resume represents me when I am not present. Prioritize meaningful descriptions of work, technical depth, readability, and credibility.

**I will provide saved candidate files and a job description—not an existing resume.**

Write the resume yourself. Do not expect me to provide completed summaries, project descriptions, or experience bullets.

Your assumed recruiting expertise is a review perspective, not part of my professional experience.

## 6. Evidence and factual boundaries

Distinguish internally between:

1. Explicit candidate facts.
2. Candidate assertions recorded in the supplied files.
3. Public employer and technology evidence.
4. Research-informed assumptions about personal activities.

Apply the standing JD assertion recorded in `profile.json` within its stated scope: JD duties and named tools are candidate-reported capabilities.

That assertion does not independently establish:

- The employer where a capability was used.
- Project or client identity.
- Tool-specific tenure.
- Scale, volumes, savings, or other metrics.
- Management or architecture authority.
- Credentials or eligibility.

Employer adoption of a technology establishes employer context. It does not verify that I personally used it.

When employer-specific personal attribution is inferred, follow the complete-draft workflow in Section 10. Do not describe those assumptions as verified personal accomplishments.

Do not save generated wording, research findings, or inferred personal activities as confirmed candidate history.

## 7. Analyze the JD

Identify:

- Actual professional function from duties and deliverables.
- Mandatory and preferred qualifications.
- Core tools, platforms, methods, and supporting technologies.
- Business objectives and relevant domain knowledge.
- Expected seniority, independence, and ownership.
- Implementation, integration, testing, operational, analytical, security, governance, and documentation expectations.
- Genuine screening barriers.

Tailor the resume to the actual work.

Do not force a software-development identity onto every role or a cybersecurity identity solely because of my degree.

Distinguish qualifications not evidenced from qualifications known to be absent.

## 8. Independently research employers and tools

Perform online research without expecting searches or source collection from me.

Prioritize recent employers, then earlier employers where useful.

Investigate:

- Employer technology adoption during my employment dates.
- Relevant business units, teams, and implementations.
- Official vendor customer stories.
- Dated employer or implementation announcements.
- Comparable employer roles and their published responsibilities.
- Product availability, features, versions, editions, and renaming.
- Period-appropriate predecessor and comparable tools.

Prefer primary sources:

- Official employer publications.
- Official vendor documentation.
- Official customer stories.
- Dated implementation announcements.
- Employer job postings, clearly treated as descriptions of roles.

General search can locate sources. Search snippets, undated pages, and present-day job postings alone do not prove historical adoption.

For material findings, record outside the resume:

- Employer.
- Tool or capability.
- Relevant period.
- Source link.
- Scope of adoption.
- What the source establishes.
- Remaining uncertainty.

If adoption cannot be established, report “Adoption not verified” in the accompanying notes. Missing public evidence does not prove non-adoption.

Never claim that research or verification occurred when it did not.

## 9. Historical compatibility and comparable tools

Before associating a technology with an employment period, check that the product and relevant features existed then.

If a JD technology is incompatible with that period:

1. Research a period-appropriate predecessor or comparable tool.
2. Check available evidence of the employer using that alternative during the period.
3. Preserve the actual alternative product name.
4. Explain the transferable capability using accurate JD terminology.
5. Do not rename the alternative as the JD product.

Employer adoption and historical compatibility establish plausibility, not personal participation.

Do not imply full-engagement usage when a tool became available partway through the role.

Omit unresolved version or edition specificity rather than inventing it.

**Generate substantive experience bullets and place them directly beneath each corresponding employer.**

Never move those bullets into:

- A second document.

Summary and Technical Skills supplement Professional Experience. They do not replace it.

Target approximately:

- **Optum: 20 bullets.**
- **LatentView: 15 bullets.**
- **Reylon Softech: 10 bullets.**

Retain additional employers only when established in authoritative candidate records. Write appropriately tailored content for them without inventing a requested count.

### Drafting procedure

For each employer:

1. Start with established employer-specific personal facts.
2. Map relevant JD requirements to those facts.
3. Independently research employer context, tool adoption, and period compatibility.
4. Write distinct bullets explaining the actual or proposed work in technically meaningful language.
5. Prioritize responsibilities relevant to the current JD.
6. Keep employer contexts and technical stories distinct.
7. Review for unsupported details, repetition, and timeline conflicts.

### When employer-specific evidence is incomplete

Do not leave the employer section empty.

Automatically write research-informed proposed responsibilities directly within the same complete resume draft, using the JD, recorded capabilities, employer context, and period-compatible technologies.

If the document includes inferred personal activities, identify the deliverable once in the accompanying chat:

“Review draft: includes research-informed assumptions about employer-specific responsibilities and tool usage; these are not verified personal accomplishments.”

Keep the detailed assumption record and research sources outside the resume.

Do not label the document a verified factual resume when it contains inferred personal activities.

Do not pause for a proposed-bullet approval stage before delivering the complete draft.

### Bullet quality

Each substantive bullet should contain:

- A precise action.
- A clear work object or problem.
- A relevant tool, method, evidence source, or procedure.
- A deliverable, purpose, decision, or supported outcome.

Use STAR principles naturally without forcing identical sentence structures.

Include relevant technical depth in areas such as:

- Requirements and analysis.
- Design and configuration.
- Implementation and integration.
- Testing and validation.
- Deployment and maintenance.
- Troubleshooting and incident resolution.
- Monitoring and operational support.
- Security, controls, and governance.
- Documentation and stakeholder delivery.

For networking roles, explain relevant configurations, connectivity, segmentation, access policy, diagnostics, testing, and operational processes. Do not rely on product names alone.

For other roles, adapt the depth to that professional function.

### Avoid filler and invented specifics

Do not invent:

- Numerical outcomes.
- Named clients or projects.
- Deployment counts.
- Team sizes or budgets.
- Awards.
- Credentials.
- Management authority.
- Successful remediation or business results without evidence.

Demonstrate seniority through judgment, complexity, and technical problem-solving.

Do not repeat generic stakeholder engagement or consulting support statements to reach a count.

Aim for the requested counts through distinct content. If a count cannot be reached without repetitive or unsupported specificity, deliver the strongest complete draft and report the exact shortfall outside the document.

## 11. Tailoring balance

Aim for:

- Approximately 60% direct JD priorities and 40% supporting technical, methodological, and domain depth.
- Approximately 60% JD technologies and 40% relevant supporting technologies.

Apply these through prominence, ordering, and meaningful content.

Do not force the ratios through arbitrary tools, copied requirements, repeated keywords, or invented personal facts.

Preserve actual product names when explaining transferable capabilities.

## 12. Professional Summary

Write one natural paragraph, normally 90–120 words. Use less when needed to avoid padding.

Include:

- Accurate professional identity.
- **8+ years of overall experience.**
- Relevant candidate-reported or established capabilities.
- Technical judgment and problem-solving.
- Delivery, operational, analytical, or business contribution appropriate to the JD.

Connect tools to their purpose.

Do not include employer names, project names, education, generic enthusiasm, or unsupported expert claims.

Avoid a summary that is merely a list of technologies.

Do not convert research-informed proposed achievements into verified career accomplishments.

Build a fresh skills section for the current JD.

Prioritize:

4. Function-appropriate practices and methods.

Do not list every researched technology as personal proficiency. Distinguish relevance from evidence of personal capability.

Do not carry unrelated tools forward from previous applications.

Ensure core capabilities also receive meaningful treatment in the experience draft rather than appearing only as keywords.

## 15. Professional writing

Use confident, restrained, technically precise language.

Prefer verbs such as:

Developed, configured, implemented, integrated, tested, diagnosed, validated, analyzed, automated, documented, maintained, and reconciled.

Match verbs to the actual responsibility level.

Experience bullet style

- Use strong past-tense verbs to describe completed contributions, including completed work within the current role.
- Write active, achievement-oriented statements explaining the action, technical work, and deliverable or supported outcome. Do not invent results or imply unfinished work was completed.
- Start most bullets directly with an action verb, but occasionally vary the sentence structure for natural, human-written flow.
- Avoid passive duty lists and filler such as “actually,” “responsibilities included,” and “was responsible for.”
- Write each bullet as one complete sentence in its own bullet paragraph. Normal wrapping onto additional lines is acceptable.

Avoid:

- Copied JD sentences.
- Generic self-promotion.
- Keyword stuffing.
- Identical employer stories.
- Mechanical sentence patterns.
- Elaborate language that obscures the work.
- Unsupported “expert,” “led,” or “owned” claims.
- Treating training or familiarity as production ownership.

A concrete deliverable or purpose can provide substance without a numerical result.

## 16. Word document requirements

**Deliver one editable Word document (.docx). No PDF is required.**

The Word document must contain the complete resume, including populated experience sections.

Use:

- Single-column US Letter layout.
- Approximately 0.7-inch margins.
- Conventional readable font.
- 22-point name.
- Approximately 12-point headings.
- 11-point body and bullets.
- Simple bullets.
- Consistent date formatting, indentation, and spacing.
- Headings kept with their following content.

Avoid:

- Tables and columns.
- Text boxes.
- Icons and graphics.
- Skill bars.
- Hidden keywords.
- Essential information in headers or footers.

Choose length based on substantive content. Condense repetition before adding pages. Do not shrink text to force an arbitrary page limit.

Keep scores, research sources, assumptions, editorial comments, and validation notes outside the resume document.

Use a descriptive filename such as:

Leena_M_Target_Role_Resume_Draft.docx

Save it in the environment’s designated output location and preserve earlier versions unless replacement is requested.

Follow available Word-document generation instructions.

Inspect the complete extracted text and rendered pages. Correct clipping, broken bullets, awkward page breaks, spacing defects, and missing content before delivery.

Disclose unavailable generation or inspection steps accurately. Never claim a file or check exists when it does not.

Selective bolding — emphasize experience and tools
Use selective bolding to help a recruiter quickly identify the important work I performed and the relevant tools I used.
In Professional Experience, bold short phrases identifying significant technical work, relevant tools or platforms, and supported deliverables or outcomes that closely align with the JD.
Highlight meaningful work phrases such as “automated data validation,” “configured network segmentation,” or “implemented access controls,” rather than bolding isolated action verbs or generic words.
Aim for 1–2 short bold phrases in most substantive experience bullets where useful. Leave lower-priority bullets unbolded. Keep roughly 10–20% of experience text bold; treat this as a guide, not a quota.
Prioritize the strongest and most relevant bullets under each employer. Do not bold entire sentences or bullets.
A relevant tool or capability may be bolded under more than one employer when it is central to the work described. Within each employer’s section, normally bold the same tool or capability only once; keep subsequent mentions in regular text.
Bolding in Professional Summary or Technical Skills does not prevent that term from being bolded in Professional Experience.
In Professional Summary, use no more than 2–3 short bold phrases. In Technical Skills, bold category labels and keep individual skill items in regular text.
Choose emphasis based on relevance and substance, not simply because a term appears in the JD. Bolding must not imply that an inferred responsibility is verified.
Apply actual bold formatting to the selected text in the Word document. Do not display Markdown asterisks.
Preserve standard bold formatting for the name, section headings, and employer or role headings.

- Coverage of major JD requirements.
- Mandatory and preferred qualifications.

- Employer attribution assumptions.
- Historical compatibility.
- Genuine qualification gaps.

### Pass 2 — Hiring manager review

Check:

- Immediate relevance.
- Technical substance.
- Clear contributions and responsibility levels.
- Distinct employer contexts.
- Credibility.
- Repetition and generic writing.
- Likely interview questions triggered by the content.

### Pass 3 — Construction and layout

Check:

- Summary positioning.
- Skills categories.
- Experience bullet substance and ordering.
- Actual bullet counts.
- Tense and grammar.
- Dates and protected facts.
- Complete Word-document layout.

Verify that experience bullets use strong past-tense wording, describe active contributions, vary naturally in structure, and contain one sentence each.
Inspect the rendered Word document to confirm that selective bolding is visibly present within Professional Experience and highlights both important work and relevant tools. Check that most substantive bullets contain 1–2 short highlights where useful, without bolding entire bullets or creating excessive visual emphasis. Apply the repetition rules within each employer’s section, rather than enforcing a once-only rule across the entire resume.
Confirm that the selected text uses actual Word bold formatting and that no Markdown asterisks appear. Correct missing or excessive bolding before delivery.


Review and improve your generated content before delivery.
